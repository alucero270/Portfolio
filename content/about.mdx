Loose Arrow Labs is a founder-led technical engineering studio operated by Alex Lucero. The studio focuses on prototype systems engineering, technical R&D, and integration work that crosses software, embedded systems, infrastructure, AI tooling, and physical hardware.

The goal is simple: build and integrate real technical systems in a way that can be inspected, tested, documented, and improved.

## Founder and Technical Lead

I am Alex Lucero. I bring eight years of professional software development experience together with hands-on mechanical, hardware, and infrastructure work. That combination matters because many useful engineering problems do not stop at a clean discipline boundary.

I have worked across application development, C#/.NET systems, C/C++, Python, Linux services, Dockerized infrastructure, AI retrieval workflows, sensor interfaces, ECU signal emulation, mechanical swaps, bench wiring, and physical iteration. Loose Arrow Labs exists to turn that range into a focused practice: multidisciplinary systems engineering and prototype development.

## What Makes This Different

This is not a web-design portfolio and it is not a scaled consultancy pretending to have a large bench. It is a founder-led engineering practice with a clear technical direction.

Loose Arrow Labs is best suited for problems where the hard part is integration:

- Embedded devices need to talk to software services
- Mechanical changes affect electronic control expectations
- AI workflows need reliable infrastructure and deterministic operating boundaries
- Telemetry, logging, and validation have to make system behavior visible
- A rough technical idea needs to become a scoped prototype with known risks

## Working Style

I start by making the system easier to reason about: interfaces, data ownership, signal paths, physical constraints, failure modes, and validation steps. The goal is not an impressive first version. The goal is a system that can be changed safely because the important assumptions are visible.

When a project touches hardware, physical constraints and testability come first. When it touches AI, retrieval quality, orchestration boundaries, and failure modes are treated like any other engineering concern. When it touches infrastructure, repeatability and operating notes are part of the deliverable.

## Current Direction

Loose Arrow Labs is in an active R&D and early consulting phase. Current work centers on complete, inspectable systems across several domains:

- **Automotive systems integration** - ECU adaptation, signal emulation, drivetrain telemetry, reverse-engineering control expectations
- **Robotics and telemetry** - MCU/Linux architectures, runtime coordination, actuator and sensor interfaces, observability
- **Embedded Linux systems** - device services, framed telemetry, persistence, transport, interface validation
- **AI infrastructure** - local inference, GPU/server lab architecture, retrieval workflows, agent tooling, AI-assisted engineering automation
- **Technical software** - APIs, internal tooling, engineering dashboards, integration services, operational interfaces
- **Infrastructure and operations** - Linux systems, networking, storage, Docker, observability, self-hosted engineering environments
- **CAD and fabrication-aware workflows** - physical iteration, 3D printing, bench fixtures, prototype packaging, mechanical constraints

The site will increasingly become an engineering notebook: architecture diagrams, subsystem breakdowns, validation notes, process logs, CAD/fabrication artifacts, and project evidence rather than broad claims.
